Dear Speaker Villagomez and President King-Nabors:
This is to inform you that I have signed into law House Bill 24-108, HS1, SD1, CCS1, entitled, "to
provide a Budget Plan of the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands for Fiscal Year 2027
and make appropriations for the operations and activities of the Government of the Commonwealth
of the Northern Mariana Islands, its agencies, instrumentalities, and independent programs, and to
provide budgetary authority for government corporations; and for other purposes." The measure
includes the appropriation for the Minimum Annual Payment to the Settlement Fund, ensuring the
continuity of pension benefit payments in Fiscal Year 2027.
Section 7(b) of the CNMI Constitution provides the Governor 20 days to consider any appropriation
bills. However, H.B. 24-108, HS1, SD1, CCS1 was received by my office just hours prior to the
opening of the new fiscal year, leaving me with insufficient time to thoroughly review the impact of
the budget provisions across all instrumentalities.
After careful consideration of our current fiscal circumstances and the changing economic conditions
facing the government, I have determined that certain provisions are no longer aligned with our fiscal
priorities or policy objectives and cannot be responsibly implemented under present conditions.
Accordingly, and after considering available alternatives, it was necessary for me to determine that a
temporary government shutdown was necessary to allow these underlying fiscal and policy concerns
to be addressed in a deliberate and lawful manner while safeguarding essential government functions
and services to the fullest extent permitted by law. Our objective is to uphold the principles of
responsible governance, restore fiscal discipline, and ensure that government operations reflect the
economic realities we face today.
Although I commend the members of both the House and Senate for their efforts in ensuring the
enactment of the Commonwealth's expenditure plan for Fiscal Year 2027, I remain hopeful that the
Legislature will act more expeditiously in the future, allowing sufficient time for the Executive to

COMMENTS ON APPROVED PARTS OF THE ACT
Section 601. Utilities.
Section 601(a) This provision requires CUC monthly utility billings for departments and agencies to
be paid by the central government. The terms "department" and "agency" are not defined, so it is
unclear whether this provision is intended to require payment for billings for the Mayors' Offices, the
Judiciary, and the Legislature.
Section 602. Hiring Restriction.
Section 602(a) provides that no new or vacant position shall be filled except positions that are
essential/critical for the delivery of public services as defined in Section 103(c). First, I note that
"essential" positions are defined in Section 103(c), while "critical" positions are defined in Section
103(d). "Critical" positions include those "as determined by the Governor." Provided that I retain the
authority to designate certain positions as critical so they may continue to be filled, this section will
not restrict the Executive Branch's ability to respond to operational needs.
In addition, Section 602(b) provides that a Notice of Personnel Action is deemed processed and filled
if a Notice of Personnel Action has been executed, a candidate has been selected, or action has been
initiated to fill a vacancy. I note that this may only be enforceable to the extent it refers to authorized
positions with adequate funding.
Section 610. OGM Funds in Excess of Office of Grants Management Budget.
Allocating excess funds received by the Office of Grants Management to the retirees' benefits is
insufficient to sustain adequate funding and operation of the program as required payments must be
made timely to avoid termination of the plan. Direct funding appropriation is necessary and should
be prioritized in the revised budget for Fiscal Year 2027.
I also note that OGM-SC is tasked by statute with assisting instrumentalities across the CNMI
government with applying for, managing, and complying with the terms and conditions of federal
grants, not only with directly administering grants itself. OGM staff should keep the Legislature and
Executive Branch abreast of their efforts to assist various agencies with audit compliance as well as
their management of grants directly administered by OGM.

Section 614. FEMA Reimbursements and Consumer Protection Settlement Funds.
Section 614(b) provides that any nonrestricted consumer protection settlement funds received by the
Government during Fiscal Year 2027 shall be reserved for appropriation by the Legislature.
I note that the Meta settlement funds cannot be considered "nonrestricted" funds as the settlement
agreement states that the funds are to be used "for any lawful purpose consistent with the Settling
State's Attorney General's authority." People of the State of California, et al. v. Meta Platforms, Inc.,
et al. (N.D. Cal. Aug. 26, 2026), ECF No. 572-1, Settlement Agreement VI.G.1.
This amount therefore will not be made available for appropriation. I anticipate that those funds will
be made available for expenditure by application to the Office of the Attorney General, in a manner
similar to how use of Purdue Pharma settlement funds is authorized.
Section 704. Legislative Branch.
Section 704(h) allows salary adjustments for staff of the Legislative Bureau and the Youth Congress
with an annual performance evaluation and consultation with the Speaker of the House and President
of the Senate, subject to funding. I note that this is inconsistent with the Legislature's intent to impose
a salary cap on Executive Branch positions, which I have disapproved as discussed below.
Section 705. Executive Branch
Section 705(e) provides compensation beyond normal operating hours, creating an inconsistency with
Schedule A, which provides compensation equivalent to only 60 hours. CIQ personnel must first
fulfill their regular 43-hour work requirement before overtime can be compensated. Accordingly,
funding should first cover the full regular work schedule before funds are made available for hours
beyond normal operating hours.
Section 708. Third Senatorial District — Mayor of Saipan; Mayor of the Northern Islands; and
Saipan and Northern Islands Municipal Council.
Section 708(b) of the Act requires $100,000 in funds to be used for typhoon recovery costs
reimbursable by FEMA. I note that the first sentence requires the funds to be used for typhoon
recovery costs reimbursable by FEMA. I recommend that the Mayors' Offices consult with the
Governor's Authorized Representative regarding which costs are reimbursable.
Section 710. Independent Programs
Section 710(c) provides that the expenditure authority for funds allocated to the Public Assistance
Office shall be the Governor's Authorized Representative, but the corresponding line item in
Schedule A has $0 appropriated to it. Reprogramming any funds into a line item for Public Assistance
matching will require subsequent amendment of the budget law.
Section 802. Non General Revenue Sources
Allocation of Commonwealth Worker Fee Funds pursuant to Section 802(b) may need to be updated
pursuant to the current year's spending plan agreed between the CNMI Department of Labor and US
Department of Labor.
Section 802(c) provides that in the event that non-general revenue funds are available to fund the
GHLI program, the funds shall be used for such program. I observe that the GHLI program should be
directly and fully funded with a dedicated line item to avoid breach of the Settlement Agreement.

VETOED PARTS OF THE ACT
Section 102. Findings and Purpose.
Section 102(c) seeks to prohibit furloughs until regulations are promulgated by the Civil Service
Commission. New proposed furlough procedures were published in the September 15, 2026 volume
of the Commonwealth Register and are pending effective promulgation. However, government
agencies already have the authority under NMIAC § 10-20.2-257 and applicable law to furlough
personnel.
Section 102(e) provides that the Commonwealth shall seek funding from various sources to fund the
Public School System. This section does not present a coherent plan for consistent allocation to the
Public School System across all four quarters of Fiscal Year 2027. In addition, the Meta settlement
funds and FEMA reimbursements are not revenue available to the legislature for appropriation.
Section 302. Prior Certification of Funds.
The second sentence of Section 302 provides that the prior certification of funds does not apply to
certain instrumentalities of the Commonwealth government. This section unreasonably permits
deficit spending without a certification of fund availability by certain instrumentalities of the
Commonwealth government but not others.
Section 404. Work Hours.
I have vetoed Section 404 because its minimum compensation requirements cannot be implemented
on October 1, 2026, without first satisfying applicable personnel, contractual, and fiscal requirements.
For civil service employees, NMIAC § 10-20.2-344 requires ten working days' advance notice when
an employee is changed from a standard workweek to a non-standard workweek. The reduction of
work hours for excepted service employees is likewise subject to the terms of applicable employment
contracts and personnel requirements. In certain cases, implementation may require advance notice,
contract modification, or other action before a reduction in hours or compensation can lawfully take
effect.
Section 404 also creates a significant cash-flow problem. The Department of Finance cannot
guarantee monthly allotments sufficient to satisfy the required compensation level if projected
revenues are not collected in full or if other budgetary obligations require the reallocation of available
resources.
The resulting fiscal impact is estimated to increase the Commonwealth's budgetary gap by
approximately $3.3 million, including the cost of maintaining the required compensation level for the
Government workforce and the 64-hour requirement applicable to the Public School System.
The underlying fiscal reality is straightforward. The Commonwealth is projected to collect
approximately $12.7 million in gross revenue each month. Approximately $4.3 million is committed
to MAP payments and other earmarked obligations, leaving approximately $8.4 million for the
operations of all branches, departments, agencies, and instrumentalities of the Commonwealth.

Those recurring revenues are insufficient to support the full level of expenditures contemplated by

additional funding sources. A budget provision cannot create revenues that have not been collected.
For these reasons, I cannot approve Section 404. The provision imposes a recurring compensation
obligation that is not presently supported by a reliable and sustainable funding source and would
materially increase the Commonwealth's projected fiscal shortfall.
Section 405. Reduction of Work Hours.
Section 405 provides that a full pay period shall be earned for purposes of annual leave, sick leave,
and other benefits, despite the implantation of austerity measures resulting in reduced work hours. I
have disapproved this section because it may be necessary for forthcoming austerity measures to
implement cuts to ancillary benefits proportionally with cuts to hours.
My administration urges the Civil Service Commission to revise existing regulations to support
prudent fiscal policy during periods of austerity. Specifically, when reductions in hours are
implemented, corresponding benefits such as sick leave and annual leave should be adjusted in
proportion to the reduced hours per pay period. This alignment will help realize meaningful cost
savings.
Further, I recommend that the annual leave accrual policy be amended to mirror the federal
government's "use-it-or-lose-it" approach, whereby unused leave expires at the end of the calendar
year. Such a change would significantly reduce the government's growing unfunded liability
associated with accumulated leave balances.
These measures are necessary to safeguard the financial health of the Commonwealth while
maintaining fairness and transparency in public service employment practices.
Section 406. Furloughs.
Section 406 states that there shall be no furloughs until rules and regulations are promulgated by the
Civil Service Commission. I have vetoed this Section because the existing regulations already
authorize furloughs, and individual appointing authorities must maintain the ability to manage their
employees to ensure they do not overspend the funds available to them for personnel.
Section 603. Salary Cap.
Section 603(a) and (b) state that subject to a Joint Resolution, all salaries must remain the same as the
listed salary in the Governor's FY2027 budget submission on July I, 2026. I have disapproved this
Section because it violates the separation of powers for the Legislature to determine via joint
resolution whether funds allocated to personnel may be reallocated within the Executive Branch to
ensure that positions essential or critical for delivery of public services can be adequately staffed and
will be problematic as it restricts the Executive Branch's ability to respond to operational needs.
Section 605. Revolving Accounts.
Section 605 requires fifty percent (50%) of all revolving accounts and special account cumulative
balances as of September 30, 2026, to be reserved for appropriation of the Legislature. I have
disapproved this Section because many of these funds have already been committed for contracts and

Section 608. Vacant Positions.
Section 608 provides that any position that becomes vacant shall be closed out. I have disapproved
this section because implementation of this provision places an inflexible constraint on the Executive
Branch's ability to respond to operational needs.
Section 614. FEMA Reimbursements and Consumer Protection Settlement Funds.
Section 614(a) provides that any cumulative underrun FEMA reimbursements received by the
Executive Branch during Fiscal Year 2027 shall be reserved for appropriation by the Legislature. I
have disapproved this Section because underrun funds are already included in Commonwealth
revenue and cannot be appropriated again.
As noted above, Meta settlement funds referenced in Section 614(b) are not "nonrestricted."
Section 704(g). Legislative Branch.
Section 704(g) allows the expenditure authority for the Legislative Bureau to have unrestricted
reprogramming authority to move funding from funding from Personnel to Operations, which
specifically includes Utilities, and from Operations to Personnel. I have disapproved the provision
authorizing unrestricted reprogramming authority to move funding from Operations to Personnel.
Funds for Utilities must be expended for Utilities to ensure that the Legislature remains current on its
utility billings as directed by ongoing federal litigation.
Section 705. Executive Branch
I am disapproving Section 705(d), which provides that the Department of Community and Cultural
Affairs (DCCA), unlike other CNMI executive agencies, would retain indirect costs from federal
grants to support its operations and utilities—costs that would otherwise be statutorily earmarked for
the Office of Grants Management. I find no sufficient policy or administrative justification for treating
DCCA differently from other executive agencies under this framework.
Accordingly, my administration will direct federally funded programs to support their operational
and utility needs through savings generated by transitioning to the lower 7.5 percent indirect cost
rate, while maintaining a consistent and equitable approach to the allocation and use of federal grant
resources across the Executive Branch.
Section 706. First Senatorial District — Mayor of Rota and Rota Municipal Council.
Section 706(b) of the Act specifically requires the Department of Finance to transfer the $100,000
appropriated to the Rota Mayor's Office for FEMA-reimbursable typhoon recovery costs to the Rota
Municipal Treasury within a reasonable period after the Act becomes law and requires such funds to
be used for typhoon recovery costs reimbursable by FEMA.
The FEMA program, however, is administered and overseen by the Public Assistance Office, under
the Governor's Authorized Representative. The Department of Finance does not have authority or
control over the FEMA reimbursement and drawdown process. Accordingly, the timing of the transfer
is dependent, in part, on the availability and receipt of the corresponding FEMA reimbursement funds.
I have disapproved the second sentence requiring transfer of $100,000 upon the Act becoming law.

Section 709. Other Programs.
Section 709(c) provides that funds allocated to the Public School System shall not be used for legal
fees including but not limited to the cost of legal representation. I have disapproved the restriction
relating to legal representation because such representation is a necessary expense of the autonomous
agencies, whether such representation is provided by outside counsel or by agreement with the Office
of the Attorney General.
Also, Section 709(c)(1) provides that of the $25,028,571 allocated to PSS, $16,177,027.52 shall be
allocated for the first two quarters of Fiscal Year 2027 to cover 64 work hours for employees and
school operations, with the understanding that the CNMI government will seek additional funds for
personnel and operations for the remainder of the fiscal year in the amount of $7,325,484.00. In
addition, Section 709(c)(2) requires a transfer of $2,696,171.25 to fund PSS's 64-hour personnel and
school operations for October 2026 through March 2027. I have disapproved these Sections for two
reasons. First, the requirement to transfer the majority of expenses in the first two quarters of the
Fiscal Year will frontload the annual appropriations and lead to a shortfall in available funding to the
extent that monthly expenditures exceed projected revenue collections. Second, the effect of these
provisions budgets a total of $32,354,055 to PSS for Fiscal Year 2027, $7,325,484 of which is
unfunded. While I appreciate the emphasis on adequately funding the Public School System, any
appropriations that exceed $12,514.285.50 in the first six months of Fiscal Year 2027 must be
accounted for through reductions to spending by other agencies and departments. The funding issue
cannot simply be deferred to the second half of the year.
In addition, Section 709(e) provides that the Commonwealth Healthcare Corporation shall administer
the Health Network Program, and that inter-island medical referral services shall include stipends,
airfare, and lodging for patients and escorts. I have disapproved the requirement to include stipends,
airfare, and lodging for patients and escorts availing of inter-island medical referral services. The

Section 710. Independent Programs.
The second paragraph of Section 710(b) requires all funds collected for services rendered by the
Office of the Attorney General to be reserved to fund the Investigative Division. 1 CMC § 7712
authorizes interagency transfers by way of reimbursements for goods and services performed by the
agency. The Attorney General must provide legal services and represent the Commonwealth, its
departments, and autonomous agencies. This obligation is set forth in Article III, Section 11 of the
NMI Constitution.
To fulfill this obligation under a limited budget, the Attorney General has entered into agreements
with various agencies to provide financial support for assigned attorneys. The Attorney General's
Office uses this support to fund attorneys' salaries and operations.
The second paragraph of Section 710(b) was vetoed as contrary to the Attorney General's
constitutional duties, 1 CMC § 7712, and conflicting with agreements between the Attorney General
and the autonomous agencies.
Section 711. Department of Public Lands.
Section 711(a) requires the Department of Public Lands to pay the Department of Lands and Natural
Resources for services provided by its personnel, and to provide payments for the landscaping and
ground maintenance of all properties that fall under the purview of Parks and Recreation. I have
disapproved the last sentence of Section 711(a), which provides for payment for ground maintenance
and landscaping work under the jurisdiction of Parks and Recreation, as such work is a statutory
responsibility of the Department of Lands and Natural Resources.
In addition, Section 711(b) states that the Department of Public Lands is authorized to expend up to
$800,000 pursuant to a Memorandum of Agreement between DPL, DLNR, and the Mayors of Rota
and Tinian & Aguiguan for maintenance and security of public lands. I have disapproved the language
referencing "up to $800,000 annually" because that language does not appear in the referenced
Memorandum of Agreement, and the authorized amount should be consistent with the amount in
DPL's approved budget.
I urge the Department of Public Lands to timely process and remit payment for invoices submitted
by the Division of Parks and Recreation within the Department of Lands and Natural Resources, for
services rendered.
I also observe that the Department of Public Lands continues to have an ongoing obligation to comply
with the provisions of Executive Order 2025-006.
Conclusion
As we implement the budget appropriations contained in this Act, I remind the Legislature of the
urgent need to enact new and sustainable sources of revenue to address the decline in revenues
forecast for Fiscal Year 2027. My Administration will take immediate action to implement
proportionate budget reductions across the Executive Branch. These measures will include a
