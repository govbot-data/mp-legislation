A BILL FOR AN ACT
To adopt the village delimitation and name master map for Saipan
as the sole village delimitation and name master map of Saipan and
to provide for the Office of the Mayor of Saipan to be the primary
custodian for maintaining and preserving the master map and for the
Department of Commerce — Central Statistics Division to be the
secondary custodian; and for other purposes.
BE IT ENACTED BY THE 24TH NORTHERN MARIANAS
COMMONWEALTH LEGISLATURE:
1 Section 1. Short Title. This Act shall be cited as the "Saipan Village
2 Delimitation and Name Master Map".
3 Section 2. Findings and Purpose. The Commonwealth Legislature
4 recognizes that the islands within the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana
5 Islands can be distinctly identified by the names of the different villages located on
6 each island. Residents and many visitors recognize they are in Saipan by the village
7 name, such as Chalan Kanoa, or they identify Tinian when an address mentions
8 Marpo, and they know they are in Rota when directions to a business lead to
9 Songsong. Every island features distinct village names that reflect its individuality.

1 The Legislature recognizes that identifying a village is essential for
2 effectively responding to emergency calls, as reaching a specific location within
3 minutes can be a matter of life and death. The legislature also recognizes that the
4 U.S. Census Bureau has reliably carried out the decennial census, as mandated by
5 Article I, § 2 of the U.S. Constitution, to gather data in the Commonwealth of the
6 Northern Mariana Islands. This includes information on the population residing in
7 each village across the three main islands of Saipan, Tinian, and Rota, as well as
8 the islands located north of Saipan. For many years, the CNMI Department of
9 Commerce — Central Statistics Division (DOC-CSD) has been responsible for
10 conducting the decennial census under the oversight of the U.S. Census Bureau,
11 utilizing a statistical map to delineate the boundaries that distinguish one village
12 from another. The statistical map also names the different areas within the
13 boundaries, assigning each area its specific village name.
14 The Legislature additionally finds that residents and businesses in the
15 CNMI are using online business transactions for purposes of ordering materials and
16 supplies from outside the CNMI. While the residents and businesses enjoy door
17 delivery of mail orders, couriers use local directions to locate a physical address,
18 which is inefficient for couriers that are concerned with the cost of delivery. The
19 Legislature further finds that another purpose of implementing the street naming
20 and addressing system is to make door delivery of mails by the United States Postal
21 Service a reality in the CNMI, a move that would ultimately reduce or even

5 The Legislature finds it imperative for a standard village identification and

1 104. Demarcation of village limits.
2 (1) The Office of the Mayor of Saipan shall solely be responsible for
3 demarcating the village limits by identifying immovable physical features
4 on the ground, for example, monuments, permanent public or private
5 structures or improvements on leased public lands, street centerlines, street
6 intersection centerlines, natural contours, e.g., top or foot of precipice, and
7 other easily identifiable physical features in the terrain. The Office of the
8 Mayor of Saipan shall record the location of the immovable, identifiable
9 physical features by using a geographic information system device capable
10 of pinpointing the exact coordinate location. In inaccessible terrains, the
11 Office of the Mayor of Saipan shall use existing data based on polygon
12 calculations to identify the point-to-point boundary limits. The Office of the
13 Mayor of Saipan shall, to the extent practicable, not use improvements or
14 structures on private land as landmarks, unless it determines that do so in
15 necessary.
16 (2) Removal of permanent improvement or structure from privately
