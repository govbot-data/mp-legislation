1 The Legislature finds that the appropriation of these funds represents a
2 targeted economic and social stabilization package for the Commonwealth. By
3 steering the $7.2 million identified in Governor's Communication (House) 24-212
4 directly into the Public School System (PSS), retiree benefits, Medicaid and the
5 restoration of public work hours, the legislature is addressing the four most
6 vulnerable pillars of the CNMI's frontline economy. First, Stabilizing the Public
7 School System. Under Section 709, PSS was left with a budget shortfall for the
8 final two quarters of the fiscal year. Appropriating these funds directly to education
9 achieves two critical outcomes: Preventing an Educational Shutdown: Without this
10 supplemental funding, PSS would be forced to aggressively truncate its school year
11 or implement an alternative calendar that could compromise instructional hours and
12 student achievement; and Securing Federal Grants: Many federal education grants
13 require a "Maintenance of Effort" (MOE) or local matching funds. Injecting local
14 revenues into PSS safeguards millions of dollars in matching federal funding that
15 would otherwise be penalized or clawed back. Second, Protecting Retiree Benefits.
16 Retirees are on fixed incomes and are the most exposed to inflationary pressures
17 and rising healthcare costs during periods of government austerity. The
18 appropriated funds will be retroactively applied thus ensuring that our retirees
19 receive their full payment beginning in October and moving forward. Preserving
20 their safety net by ensuring that pension payments, healthcare premiums, and
21 promised benefits remain funded prevents an immediate spike in poverty among

9 to fully fund and expand these hours to build a stronger, more stable future for our
10 public servants and their families. And lastly, funding the Commonwealth
11 Medicaid Agency. Appropriating local matching funds to the Commonwealth
12 Medicaid Agency provides an unmatched financial return for the CNMI's
13 healthcare ecosystem: For every local dollar the CNMI allocates to Medicaid, the
14 federal government matches it with a significantly higher percentage through the
15 Federal Medical Assistance Percentage. Failing to fund CMA's local match would
16 cause the Commonwealth to forfeit millions of dollars in federal healthcare
17 subsidies. The Commonwealth Healthcare Corporation relies heavily on Medicaid
18 reimbursements to cover its operating costs. Ensuring CMA has the necessary local
19 funds allows it to pay CHCC and private providers promptly, preventing hospital
20 cash-flow shortages, staffing cuts, or service reductions. Just as important is
21 Protecting Frontline Patient Care. Medicaid serves the most financially vulnerable
