1 Section 1. Findings and Purpose.
2 The Legislature finds and declares as follows:
3 (a) Modernization and Alignment of Commonwealth Lottery Law with the Reality of the
4 Internet and International Digital Markets.
5 The existing Commonwealth Lottery Commission Act, codified at 1 CMC §§ 9301
6 through 9323, was enacted by Public Law 3-60 in 1983 and was drafted at a time when all lottery
7 activity in the Commonwealth, and substantially all lottery activity in the world, was conducted
8 exclusively through the sale of paper tickets at fixed, physical retail locations. The drafters of the
9 1983 Act could not have anticipated, and the 1983 Act accordingly does not address, the
10 emergence of a global digital economy built on the World Wide Web, in which lawful, regulated
11 online lottery operations now generate more than one hundred to one hundred twenty billion
12 dollars ($100B - $120B) in annual revenue worldwide and are projected to grow at compound
13 annual rates approaching ten percent through the end of the current decade.

6 (c) Clear Statutory Distinction Between Public Lottery and Global Digital Lottery.
7 The Legislature finds that the Global Digital Lottery is categorically different from the traditional
8 "Public Lottery" regulated under the existing Commonwealth Lottery Commission Act, for the
9 following reasons:
10 Participant Base. The Public Lottery is available to residents of, and persons physically
11 present within, the Commonwealth. The Global Digital Lottery is categorically prohibited from
12 accepting any participation by Commonwealth residents, by any other United States citizen, or by
13 any person physically present within the United States or its territories.
14 Operations and Infrastructure. The Public Lottery is provided primarily for Commonwealth
15 residents exclusively through paper tickets or video tickets at retail locations in the
16 Commonwealth. The Public Lottery relies on physical equipment, point-of-sale video terminals,
17 paper tickets, ticket dispensers, and fixed retail premises, all of which require an extensive, locally-
18 focused, regulatory regime to manage. By contrast, the Global Digital Lottery targets a global
19 population that excludes United States citizens and residents (and thus Commonwealth citizens
20 and residents) and operates exclusively through internet and mobile-device-based digital channels,
21 with no physical retail locations in the Commonwealth and no paper tickets. The Global Digital
4

1 Lottery relies on well-developed, regulated cloud-based digital infrastructure, real-time
2 geolocation and identity-verification systems, and global payment-processing networks.
3 Economic Model. The Public Lottery serves a local market and generates local revenue
4 through ticket sales. The Global Digital Lottery serves a global market of non-United States
5 persons and generates international revenue that the Commonwealth repatriates through taxes on
6 the Licensee's Net Lottery Revenue and prize winnings.
7 Regulatory Requirements. The Public Lottery is governed by the existing 1983 statutes, as
8 amended, and implementing rules and regulations tailored to retail, physical-location operations.
9 The Global Digital Lottery requires an entirely new framework tailored to digital operations,
10 international markets, real-time compliance monitoring, and the special U.S. federal compliance
11 obligations that apply to cross-border online gaming.
12 By reason of these categorical differences, the Legislature finds that it is both appropriate and
13 necessary to establish separate and distinct statutory frameworks for the two forms of lottery
14 activity, and to expressly provide that the rules and regulations governing one framework shall not
15 apply to the other.

3 The Unlawful Internet Gambling Enforcement Act of 2006, 31 U. S. C. §§ 5361-5367
4 ("UIGEA"), restricts certain forms of internet-based wagering and payment activity. More
5 specifically, UIGEA defines "unlawful Internet gambling" as "to place, receive, or otherwise
6 knowingly transmit a bet or wager by any means which involves the use, at least in part, of the
7 Internet where such bet or wager is unlawful under any applicable Federal or State law in the State
8 or Tribal lands in which the bet or wager is initiated, received, or otherwise made." 31 U. S. C.
9 § 5362(10)(A). In the context of this section of UIGEA, the Commonwealth is a "State." Given
10 this definition, UIGEA then specifically criminalizes knowingly accepting, in "connection with
11 the participation of another person in unlawful Internet gambling," various forms of payments for
12 wagers, such as credit, electronic funds transfer, checks, or other financial instruments.
13 After considering the statutory text and available legal authorities, the Legislature adopts the good-
14 faith legal position that the U. S. Federal prohibition is not an absolute prohibition on lotteries
15 themselves, but rather a prohibition on businesses that accept any form of payments for wagers in
16 "unlawful Internet gambling," and whether the gambling is unlawful is determined by the law of
17 the jurisdiction in which the wager is received. Accordingly, the Legislature adopts the good-faith
18 legal position that since the Commonwealth, by this Lottery Modernization, expressly authorizes
19 receipt by the Licensee of wagers initiated by non-United States persons in foreign jurisdictions,
20 such wagers are not "unlawful under any applicable. . . State law in the State ... in which the bet
21 or wager is ... received," and thus, should not constitute "unlawful Internet gambling" or

11.2. Written Notice and Cure. Before commencing non-emergency enforcement, the
Commission shall deliver written notice identifying the specific provision allegedly breached, the
material facts supporting the allegation, the required corrective action, and the basis for concluding
that the alleged breach is material. Licensee shall have not fewer than ninety (90) days after receipt

11.3. Non-Emergency Remedy. After expiration of the Cure Period, the Commission may
commence a contested-case proceeding only if the alleged breach remains uncured and the
Commission reasonably concludes that Cause exists. The Commission shall employ the least
restrictive measure that addresses the uncured material breach. Except for a lawful Emergency
Protective Measure, a final non-emergency suspension, revocation, or material operational
restriction is automatically stayed for ninety (90) days after service of the final agency decision.
Licensee is entitled to expedited judicial review, and the reviewing court may extend the stay
through final resolution upon a showing of likely success on the merits and irreparable harm, after
considering the public interest, as provided in the Act.

Protective Measure only upon the written findings and on the grounds authorized by the Act. A
measure shall be limited to the particular Game, draw, feature, payment function, geographic

upon the further statutory finding that no targeted measure reasonably protects against the threat.
